Why it's here: Choose this when you expect to take unhooks and help the rescued teammate return to useful play, rather than leaving them injured.

How to Play

Opening

Check the highlighted generator cluster and a usable escape route before settling into repair. Reassess your job at each hook or chase change.

Situation

Nobody needs an immediate rescue and you are free to repair.

Do this

Work on an accessible highlighted generator that helps break up the cluster. If the Killer makes it unsafe, rotate rather than feeding a hit for the highlight.

Why

Objective information should remove indecision, not force commitment to a defended location.

Enabled by

Déjà Vu

Situation

The Killer is closing in and you have a usable vault with Lithe available.

Do this

Choose your next route before vaulting, then spend the escape on distance to that route instead of circling back into the same pressure.

Why

A mobility tool buys useful time only when you have somewhere to go.

Enabled by

Lithe

Situation

A teammate is hooked and you chose Kindred.

Do this

Check who is already moving toward the hook. Keep repairing if the rescue is covered; approach if it is not, adjusting for any Killer information you can see.

Why

Sending everyone to one rescue abandons the other jobs this loadout is meant to cover.

Enabled by

Kindred

Situation

You made the unhook and chose We'll Make It.

Do this

Offer the rescued teammate a heal where the Killer cannot immediately interrupt. If the Killer returns, move apart and escape instead of insisting on finishing under pressure.

Why

The recovery is valuable when it gets a teammate back into the Trial, not when it creates two vulnerable targets.

Enabled by

We'll Make It

Situation

You have just been unhooked and the Killer is still pursuing you.

Do this

Prioritize an escape route and be ready to hit the Skill Check if the Killer picks you up while Will to Live is active. Once the pursuit ends, return to a useful job deliberately rather than treating the protection as permanent.

Why

Its limited post-unhook opportunity cannot support an entire match plan; check its availability before relying on it.

Enabled by

Will to Live

When to stop

  • If a teammate has already covered the rescue or reset you were approaching, stop duplicating it and take the unattended generator or other open job.
  • When your safety perk is unavailable, especially after the gates are powered, choose routes and rescues on their own merits rather than banking on a second escape.
Perk details

Lithe

Lithe

What it does: Whenever you perform a Rushed Vault action while not Exhausted, gain 50% Haste for 3 seconds. Lithe cannot be used while Exhausted and causes Exhausted for 60/50/40 seconds by tier.

Activation:

  • Perform a Rushed Vault action.
  • Not be suffering from Exhausted.
  • Ends/deactivates when: The Haste effect ends after 3 seconds; Exhausted prevents triggering Lithe again until it expires.

Owner: Feng Min

Why it is here: Turn a usable vault into a route away from the current tile, so one chase does not consume every nearby resource.

Déjà Vu

Déjà Vu

What it does: The Auras of the three closest Generators that are grouped together are revealed to you. You repair those Generators 4/5/6% faster.

Activation:

  • Ends/deactivates when: Highlighted Generators update as the closest grouped trio changes.

Owner: General

Why it is here: Give yourself an objective target when nobody is calling generators, instead of leaving the dangerous cluster until last.

Will to Live

Will to Live

What it does: After you are unhooked or unhook yourself, the perk activates for 40/50/60 seconds. If the Killer grabs or picks you up during that window, succeed at the Skill Check to escape their grasp and stun the Killer for 4 seconds. Using the perk makes you the next Obsession and disables the perk for the rest of the Trial. It deactivates when the Exit Gates are powered or when you perform a Conspicuous Action. It also increases your chance of being the initial Obsession by 100% of the default value.

Activation:

  • Be unhooked or unhook yourself.
  • Be grabbed or picked up by the Killer during the 40/50/60-second activation window and succeed at the Skill Check.
  • Ends/deactivates when: Deactivates when Exit Gates are powered.
  • Ends/deactivates when: Deactivates if you perform a Conspicuous Action.
  • Ends/deactivates when: After use, disabled for the remainder of the Trial.

Owner: General

Why it is here: Keep a personal answer to being picked up again after an unhook; it is insurance for that situation, not permission to take unnecessary hits.

Kindred

Kindred

What it does: While you are hooked, all Survivors see each other's Auras, and while the Killer is within 14/15/16 metres of your Hook, all Survivors see the Killer's Aura. While another Survivor is hooked, you see the Auras of the other Survivors, and while the Killer is within 14/15/16 metres of that hooked Survivor, you see the Killer's Aura.

Activation:

  • Any Survivor is hooked.
  • Ends/deactivates when: The effect ends when no Survivor remains hooked.

Owner: General

Why it is here: Choose this when knowing whether another teammate is approaching the hook is more valuable than committing blindly yourself.

We'll Make It

We'll Make It

What it does: Whenever you unhook a Survivor, for 70/80/90 seconds you heal other Survivors 100% faster.

Activation:

  • Unhook another Survivor.
  • Ends/deactivates when: The effect ends after 70/80/90 seconds.

Owner: General

Why it is here: Choose this when you expect to take unhooks and help the rescued teammate return to useful play, rather than leaving them injured.

Solo Q and SWF differences

Solo Q

Use visible teammate activity and hook information to choose an uncovered job; the Intent System helps but does not guarantee that someone will complete it.

Coordinated SWF

Call the job you are taking before rotating, so flexibility fills a gap instead of pulling a second player off the same generator.

Weaknesses

  • You still need teammates to convert your chase time into progress; flexibility cannot supply all four players' work.

Common mistakes

  • Vaulting early without an actual chase, then planning an escape around an unavailable Lithe.
  • Treating the fourth slot as both information and healing at once; play around the option you equipped.
  • Repairing, healing, cleansing, or taking another Conspicuous Action, then assuming Will to Live remains active against a returning Killer.

Short Description

Stay useful without knowing teammates/builds/Killer. Diversification is intentional, not lack of build identity.

Overview

Solo-Q Generalist is a deliberately diversified construction philosophy rather than one narrow mechanic. Its primary role is Flex, with secondary emphasis on Info, chase, objectives, rescue. The canonical reason players run it is Resilience to uncertainty and bad role allocation.

How It Works

Early Game

Use the diversified shell to avoid an early role failure: you have at least one tool for mobility, information or objectives, and personal safety rather than assuming random teammates will cover those jobs.

Midgame

Lean into whichever function the lobby is failing to supply. The strategy is strongest when flexibility prevents dead time, duplicated rescues, bad generator routing, or a complete lack of anti-tunnel protection.

Endgame

Use the remaining flex value on the actual endgame problem rather than preserving a preplanned role. The point of the shell is independent coverage under uncertainty.

Why It Works

The strategy works by turning Flex into a favorable exchange of match resources. Its defining mechanisms are One answer to several independent failure modes. The payoff the research is trying to capture is Resilience to uncertainty and bad role allocation. The useful question is not whether the effect looks strong in isolation, but whether the time, positioning, item charges, health states, or perk slots spent to obtain it are repaid by the resulting generator, chase, rescue, or survival tempo.

Winning and Losing States

Winning state: The strategy is generating more team value than it costs in setup, travel, health states, item charges, or perk slots. Its intended payoff is being realized: Resilience to uncertainty and bad role allocation.

Losing state: The player keeps paying the setup cost after the Killer, map, teammates, or game state have made the activation low-value. That is where specialization turns into opportunity cost.

Solo Q

The canonical Solo Q evaluation is S / 90 with MEDIUM confidence. That places it as top-tier in this environment; teammate uncertainty reduces some of its ceiling; its value comes partly from reducing uncertainty without relying on voice comms. Power is a comparative research score, not an observed escape percentage.

Coordinated SWF

The canonical Coordinated SWF evaluation is B / 77 with MEDIUM confidence. That places it as viable and above average in this environment; planned teammate behavior reduces wasted commitment. Power is a comparative research score, not an observed escape percentage.

Solo Q vs SWF

SWF Power is 77 versus 90 in Solo Q. Much of the strategy's value comes from solving uncertainty that voice communication already removes, so coordinated teams need less of it.

Skill and Execution

Stage 3A does not contain canonical numeric Skill Floor, Skill Ceiling, or Execution Reliability fields, so this page does not invent them. Practically, the strategy asks the player to develop: role switching, macro awareness, recognizing what the team is currently missing, pre-positioning.

Dependencies and Reliability

  • OPPONENT_BEHAVIOR: The Killer can change the strategy's value by refusing the interaction, changing targets, or altering timing.
  • POSITIONING: The player must already be in the right place, or move there efficiently, before the value window closes.

Common Mistakes

  • Reacting only after the key event starts instead of pre-positioning for it.
