How to Play

Opening

While approaching a chase area, identify one pallet that buys the next position and one exit from the tile. Reserve the resource for a route-changing drop rather than spending it to prove the build is active.

Situation

You are inside the conditions for Five Moves Ahead and need a pallet route.

Do this

Choose the closest pallet that leads to a real next position, then keep the exit in mind before taking the interaction.

Why

The perk supplies pallet information during its active conditions; the value is route selection, not a reason to drop immediately.

Enabled by

Five Moves Ahead

Situation

The Killer reaches a pallet and the drop can create distance or protect the next objective route.

Do this

Drop only when the resulting distance or denial is worth the resource. Move through the planned exit and reassess after the perk cooldown begins.

Why

A pallet spent only for an aura or movement trigger leaves the team with less future safety.

Enabled by

Five Moves Ahead

Situation

A dropped pallet is useful again and Any Means Necessary can be completed without giving the Killer a free hit.

Do this

Reset it only after checking the Killer's location and the team's next route. If the interaction is contested, leave and keep the objective moving.

Why

Resetting restores a resource only when the time and exposure do not cost more than the pallet returns.

Enabled by

Any Means Necessary

Situation

The Killer breaks or damages a pallet, and Alert reveals the Killer's Aura.

Do this

Use the aura cue to update the route and tell teammates which resource is being removed. Do not interpret it as a pallet or window map.

Why

Alert reports Killer Break or Damage actions; the information is useful because it changes resource state.

Enabled by

Alert

Situation

The route is depleted, the map gives no useful pallet or the Killer has already cleared the area.

Do this

Leave the resource plan and take the best remaining chase path, generator or rescue job instead of travelling back to defend an exhausted tile.

Why

The strategy wins by avoiding dead zones, not by maintaining a route that no longer exists.

When to stop

  • Abort a reset when the Killer can reach you before it completes or when the restored pallet does not protect a useful route.
  • Stop routing around a spent area when ordinary objective or rescue work now repays more team tempo.
Perk details

Five Moves Ahead

Five Moves Ahead

What it does: While you are inside the Killer's Terror Radius or being chased by the Killer, you see the Auras of the 5 closest Pallets. After you drop a Pallet, you start moving 50% earlier. Dropping a Pallet puts the perk on a 40/35/30-second cooldown.

Activation:

  • Be inside the Killer's Terror Radius or be chased by the Killer to see the 5 closest Pallets and receive the Pallet-drop movement benefit.
  • Ends/deactivates when: The Aura and movement effects are unavailable while the perk is on its 40/35/30-second cooldown.
  • Ends/deactivates when: Outside the Killer's Terror Radius and chase, the active effects are unavailable.

Owner: Kwon Tae-Young

Why it is here: When Terror Radius or chase conditions are active, read nearby Pallets and use the post-drop movement to leave toward the next route.

Any Means Necessary

Any Means Necessary

What it does: The Auras of dropped Pallets are revealed to you. While standing beside a dropped Pallet, hold the Active Ability button to reset it upright over 5/4/3 seconds.

Activation:

  • Stand beside a dropped Pallet and hold the Active Ability button.
  • Ends/deactivates when: Reset interaction ends if interrupted.

Owner: Yui Kimura

Why it is here: Make a reset decision for a dropped pallet only when the interaction is safe and the restored route will matter to the team.

Alert

Alert

What it does: Whenever the Killer performs a Break or Damage Action, their Aura is revealed to you for 3/4/5 seconds.

Activation:

  • The Killer performs a Break or Damage Action.
  • Ends/deactivates when: Aura reveal ends after the tier duration.

Owner: Feng Min

Why it is here: Use a Break or Damage aura reveal to decide whether the Killer is clearing your route or has moved away; it does not reveal every pallet.

Solo Q and SWF differences

Solo Q

Treat a reset as your own timing decision and return to the next job when no teammate is visibly using the recovered route.

Coordinated SWF

Call the pallet area being spent or reset, then release the assignment so another player can keep the next generator or rescue covered.

Strengths

  • Avoid dead zones and waste fewer pallets when the map supplies a usable route.

Weaknesses

  • Map RNG and rapid Killer resource destruction can remove the activation path.
  • A reset interaction and a pallet drop both spend time or exposure that may not be repaid.

Killer counterplay

  • The Killer can clear resources quickly, refuse the expected pallet interaction or change timing so a reset becomes unsafe.

Common mistakes

  • Dropping a pallet for the perk trigger without planning the next route.
  • Starting a reset while the Killer can interrupt it or while another job is more urgent.
  • Treating Five Moves Ahead as window information or Alert as a complete resource map.

Short Description

Preserve, identify or maximize pallet resources. Pallet economy rather than generic looping.

Overview

Pallet / Resource Specialist is a strategy organized around a specific mechanic or interaction. Its primary role is Chase, with secondary emphasis on Team resource management. The canonical reason players run it is Avoid dead zones and waste fewer pallets.

How It Works

  • Activation: Create or recognize the state in which preserve, identify or maximize pallet resources. Pallet economy rather than generic looping.
  • Use case: Commit when the likely payoff, avoid dead zones and waste fewer pallets, is worth the time and slot cost.
  • Payoff: Successful execution changes the match through Pallet location/state, resetting/reuse.
  • Failure state: The plan fails when its activation is denied, arrives too late, or consumes more team tempo than the resulting benefit returns.

Why It Works

The strategy works by turning Chase into a favorable exchange of match resources. Its defining mechanisms are Pallet location/state, resetting/reuse. The payoff the research is trying to capture is Avoid dead zones and waste fewer pallets. The useful question is not whether the effect looks strong in isolation, but whether the time, positioning, item charges, health states, or perk slots spent to obtain it are repaid by the resulting generator, chase, rescue, or survival tempo.

Winning and Losing States

Winning state: The strategy is generating more team value than it costs in setup, travel, health states, item charges, or perk slots. Its intended payoff is being realized: Avoid dead zones and waste fewer pallets.

Losing state: The player keeps paying the setup cost after the Killer, map, teammates, or game state have made the activation low-value. That is where specialization turns into opportunity cost.
