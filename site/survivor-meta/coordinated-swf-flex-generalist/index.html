Player Guide

Coordinated SWF Flex Generalist

Agree who handles chase, critical generators, recovery and flexible support, then reassign the work when pressure changes.

Well-supported · Re-Forming

Verdict

Use this as a four-player plan with shared assignments and callouts.

Solo Q: Not applicable to Solo Q: this plan depends on the group distributing tools and responsibilities together.

Coordinated SWF: Choose complementary jobs before the Trial, while keeping each player able to survive a chase. Update assignments when the original specialist is occupied.

Main weakness: The plan loses value when teammates cannot communicate changes or keep following an assignment that the Trial has made unusable.

Solo QNot a queue recommendationNot a queue recommendation
Coordinated SWFS tier

Roles

No single four-perk loadout is implied; assign the role that covers the team's current gap.

Chase and pressure

Take pressure away from the generators the team has chosen, and call your route and spent resources. If the Killer leaves, return to useful work rather than chasing them to preserve the runner title.

No required perks

Critical generators

Identify the generator that matters to the team's next rotation and call the target. Agree any item investment with the group before committing; if you are chased, hand the objective to the flex player.

Field Guide recommendation

  • Déjà Vu — Give the assigned objective player a concrete cluster to discuss and break up. Share the target with teammates so their work supports the same plan.

Hook states and recovery

Track who needs the rescue and who can safely take it. Discuss a hook-state transfer before committing, including who covers you afterward; otherwise take the ordinary rescue or recovery job that is available.

Field Guide recommendation

  • Shoulder the Burden — Offer a deliberate way to redistribute hook pressure when preserving the teammate matters more than the cost to you. Agree an exit and cover for the transfer instead of treating it as a free unhook.

Save or second objective

Keep a second objective moving unless a reachable save or uncovered rescue is worth leaving it. Call which gap you are filling and return to repair when the opportunity disappears.

No required perks

Team framework

  • Runner / pressure absorber: 2 chase + 1 anti-tunnel + flex
  • Critical-gen specialist: Deja Vu + item engine + safety
  • Hook-state / reset player: Shoulder the Burden + reset/anti-camp support + safety
  • Flex saver / second objective player: saver engine + generator/chase flex

How to Play

Opening

Before the Trial, agree these four assignments, the tools each player is bringing and who covers a job when its owner is chased. Share item plans without assuming the role labels prescribe four fixed builds.

The objective player has identified the highlighted generator cluster and the team is free to act.

Situation: The objective player has identified the highlighted generator cluster and the team is free to act.

Action: Call a target and who will work it. Keep the runner's route away from that work, and let the flex player cover another useful objective or the needed rescue.

Why: Information becomes team value when it changes where people work, rather than sending everyone to the same place.

Relevant when: the Critical generators role, Déjà Vu

The Killer starts chasing the critical-generator player.

Situation: The Killer starts chasing the critical-generator player.

Action: Call the chase and let the flex player take the abandoned objective if it remains workable. The former runner should cover another open job instead of demanding that the Killer switch back.

Why: Assignments preserve coverage only if the team replaces the player who is now occupied.

Relevant when: the Critical generators role, the Save or second objective role, the Chase and pressure role

A teammate is hooked and the reset player is considering Shoulder the Burden.

Situation: A teammate is hooked and the reset player is considering Shoulder the Burden.

Action: Check that the perk is available and you are eligible, then agree who covers your exit and the remaining work. Use the transfer unhook only if accepting its hook-state and injury cost helps the team; otherwise choose an ordinary rescue plan.

Why: Moving hook pressure can protect one teammate while making the rescuer more vulnerable. The follow-up belongs in the decision, before the transfer.

Relevant when: the Hook states and recovery role, Shoulder the Burden

The reset player has used the transfer and now needs space.

Situation: The reset player has used the transfer and now needs space.

Action: Let the flex player cover the next exposed rescue or support job as needed. Do not gather for a heal that Broken currently prevents; use the available time on objectives or positioning instead.

Why: Keeping the same player responsible for every rescue ignores the cost the team just asked them to take.

Relevant when: the Hook states and recovery role, the Save or second objective role, Shoulder the Burden

The flex player has approached a save but the Killer denies the opening.

Situation: The flex player has approached a save but the Killer denies the opening.

Action: Call the cancellation and return to the uncovered objective or plan the hook rescue. Keep the other players on their jobs instead of drawing the whole team into the failed attempt.

Why: Flexible support should fill a gap without multiplying the cost of a missed window.

Relevant when: the Save or second objective role

The remaining work has changed from generator pressure to a rescue or escape.

Situation: The remaining work has changed from generator pressure to a rescue or escape.

Action: Name who handles the gate, who takes the rescue and who can absorb pressure. Reassign the former generator specialist rather than leaving them waiting for a job that no longer exists.

Why: The team's remaining win condition matters more than its opening role labels.

When to stop

  • If a player cannot perform the assigned job because of chase, hook state or unavailable tools, call the handoff immediately; do not leave the rest of the team waiting for the original plan.
  • If several players start answering the same save or rescue, confirm one approach and send the others back to the uncovered work.
Perk details

Déjà Vu

Déjà Vu

What it does: The Auras of the three closest Generators that are grouped together are revealed to you. You repair those Generators 4/5/6% faster.

Activation:

  • Ends/deactivates when: Highlighted Generators update as the closest grouped trio changes.

Owner: General

Why it is here: Give the assigned objective player a concrete cluster to discuss and break up. Share the target with teammates so their work supports the same plan.

Shoulder the Burden

Shoulder the Burden

What it does: Once per Trial, while you have fewer than 2 Hook States and are next to a hooked Survivor, use Active Ability Button 2 to unhook them. They lose 1 Hook State; you gain 1 Hook State, scream, become Injured, and become Broken for 160/140/120 seconds.

Activation:

  • Once per Trial, have fewer than 2 Hook States.
  • Stand next to a hooked Survivor and use Active Ability Button 2 to unhook them.
  • Ends/deactivates when: After use, the perk cannot be used again by that Survivor during the Trial.
  • Ends/deactivates when: Broken ends after 160/140/120 seconds.

Owner: Taurie Cain

Why it is here: Offer a deliberate way to redistribute hook pressure when preserving the teammate matters more than the cost to you. Agree an exit and cover for the transfer instead of treating it as a free unhook.

Solo Q and SWF differences

Solo Q

Intent signals do not turn one uncoordinated Survivor into this four-player allocation plan. Use an individual strategy when you cannot agree responsibilities.

Coordinated SWF

Keep callouts actionable: target generator, chase route, rescue owner and whether a save is still on. Agree a replacement whenever an assignment changes.

Weaknesses

  • The team spends attention on coordination, and a missed handoff can leave a critical generator or rescue uncovered.

Common mistakes

  • Treating the four assignments as permanent even after their owners are chased or lose their tools.
  • Equipping redundant narrow tools without checking who already covers that job.
  • Sending everyone to help the designated saver instead of maintaining objective pressure.

Short Description

Cover essentials across four players while retaining personal chase value. Unit of optimization is partly the four-player team, not one Survivor.

Overview

Coordinated SWF Flex Generalist is a deliberately diversified construction philosophy rather than one narrow mechanic. Its primary role is Flex, with secondary emphasis on Any. The canonical reason players run it is Avoid redundant team tools while preserving flexibility.

How It Works

Team Setup

Distribute responsibilities across the four-player group before and during the match: chase pressure, critical-generator work, hook-state/reset support, and flexible save/objective coverage. Avoid paying for the same narrow tool four times when voice communication can replace redundancy.

Midgame Role Switching

Treat roles as assignments, not prisons. When the Killer, hook states, or generator layout changes, shift the flex player or second objective player into the job the team currently lacks. The value comes from coverage with low redundancy.

Endgame

Collapse the architecture around the remaining win condition. Generator specialists may become rescue or gate players, while the runner or saver may absorb pressure. The team-level plan matters more than preserving each player's opening role.

Why It Works

The strategy works by turning Flex into a favorable exchange of match resources. Its defining mechanisms are Build slots chosen relative to teammates rather than independently. The payoff the research is trying to capture is Avoid redundant team tools while preserving flexibility. The useful question is not whether the effect looks strong in isolation, but whether the time, positioning, item charges, health states, or perk slots spent to obtain it are repaid by the resulting generator, chase, rescue, or survival tempo.

Winning and Losing States

Winning state: The strategy is generating more team value than it costs in setup, travel, health states, item charges, or perk slots. Its intended payoff is being realized: Avoid redundant team tools while preserving flexibility.

Losing state: The player keeps paying the setup cost after the Killer, map, teammates, or game state have made the activation low-value. That is where specialization turns into opportunity cost.
