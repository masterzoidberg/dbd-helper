Shoulder the Burden

What it does: Once per Trial, transfer one Hook State from a hooked teammate to yourself, becoming injured and temporarily Broken.

Why it's here: Once per Trial, while you have fewer than 2 Hook States and are next to a hooked Survivor, unhook them to remove 1 Hook State from them and take 1 yourself; you scream, become Injured and suffer Broken for 160/140/120 seconds.

How to Play

Opening

Before approaching the hook, compare the target's immediate elimination risk with the hook state, injury and Broken period you will assume. The transfer is valuable when it preserves the fourth player and leaves the team with a workable next rotation.

Situation

A teammate is hooked and their hook-state risk is materially higher than the cost of your own transfer.

Do this

Confirm your own Hook State count, the target's remaining state and the team's exit route before committing. If the target is not the player whose removal would most damage the team, keep the action available.

Why

The strategy is a hook-economy trade, so target selection is the central decision rather than an automatic rescue shortcut.

Enabled by

Shoulder the Burden

Situation

You have fewer than 2 Hook States, are next to the hooked Survivor and the transfer is worth its cost.

Do this

Use Active Ability Button 2 once to unhook the target. Expect the target to lose 1 Hook State and you to gain 1, scream, become Injured and become Broken; immediately take the planned exit rather than treating the state trade as free.

Why

These are the live 10.2.0 consequences of Shoulder the Burden; the PTB-wide deactivation for other Survivors is not part of the live record.

Enabled by

Shoulder the Burden

Situation

The transfer has succeeded and the Killer can now punish the user or the team is missing an objective/rescue role.

Do this

Leave the hook area, communicate the new hook-state ownership and route toward the highest-value safe job. Do not take a second unnecessary trade while already carrying the transferred state.

Why

The payoff is preserving a player, but the user has also become the vulnerable player and the perk cannot be reused during the Trial.

Situation

You already have 2 Hook States, the target is no longer in the relevant danger window, the Killer has moved the interaction out of reach or the team cannot afford your resulting Broken state.

Do this

Do not activate the perk. Switch to the ordinary rescue, objective or chase route and preserve the unused transfer for a later high-value hook state.

Why

The live activation is state- and position-gated, and the source warns that poor target selection converts the protection into cost.

When to stop

  • Abort the transfer when the target's death-prevention value has passed, a safer rescue has already resolved the danger or the target is no longer next to the hook interaction.
  • After a successful transfer, abort further hook-state trading until the team has reassessed the user's new state, injury and Broken duration.

Solo Q and SWF differences

Solo Q

Treat the transfer as a personal high-commitment call: verify the target's danger and your own Hook State count before acting, then take responsibility for the exit after the trade.

Coordinated SWF

Name the target, transfer owner and post-trade job before the unhook so the team does not duplicate rescue travel or leave the transferred player without an exit.

Strengths

  • Can preserve a vulnerable fourth player by moving hook-state risk instead of accepting an early elimination.

Weaknesses

  • The user pays a Hook State, injury and long Broken period, and the once-per-Trial action has no value if the target selection is poor.
  • The timing and teammate-state dependencies make an unannounced Solo Q transfer easier to waste.

Killer counterplay

  • The Killer can change targets, leave the hook, force a poor approach or let the user absorb the transferred state and Broken period without granting a favorable follow-up.

Common mistakes

  • Activating the transfer whenever a hooked Survivor is nearby instead of comparing hook-state value and the user's own cost.
  • Forgetting that the live user becomes Injured and Broken and gains a Hook State after the unhook.
  • Taking the transfer without calling the post-trade route, leaving the preserved player or the new target of pressure unsupported.

Short Description

Keep a vulnerable teammate alive by redistributing hook economy. Directly moves hook-state risk between players.

Overview

Hook-State Transfer is a portable package that can attach to several broader builds. Its primary role is Death prevention, with secondary emphasis on Bodyblocking, anti-tunnel. The canonical reason players run it is Preserve fourth player and deny early elimination.

How It Works

  • Activation: Create or recognize the state in which keep a vulnerable teammate alive by redistributing hook economy. Directly moves hook-state risk between players..
  • Use case: Commit when the likely payoff, preserve fourth player and deny early elimination, is worth the time and slot cost.
  • Payoff: Successful execution changes the match through Remove target hook state and assume one yourself.
  • Failure state: The plan fails when its activation is denied, arrives too late, or consumes more team tempo than the resulting benefit returns.

Why It Works

The strategy works by turning Death prevention into a favorable exchange of match resources. Its defining mechanisms are Remove target hook state and assume one yourself. The payoff the research is trying to capture is Preserve fourth player and deny early elimination. The useful question is not whether the effect looks strong in isolation, but whether the time, positioning, item charges, health states, or perk slots spent to obtain it are repaid by the resulting generator, chase, rescue, or survival tempo.

Winning and Losing States

Winning state: The strategy is generating more team value than it costs in setup, travel, health states, item charges, or perk slots. Its intended payoff is being realized: Preserve fourth player and deny early elimination.

Losing state: The player keeps paying the setup cost after the Killer, map, teammates, or game state have made the activation low-value. That is where specialization turns into opportunity cost.
