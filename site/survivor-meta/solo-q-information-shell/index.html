Player Guide

Solo-Q Information Shell

Replace missing Solo-Q calls with specific hook, objective and approach cues, then act before the information loses value.

Solo QA tierRecommended
Coordinated SWFC tier

Main weakness: Information slots can crowd out direct tempo, especially when team calls already answer the same questions.

Recommended Build

Field Guide recommendation

  • Kindred · Core — Hook events expose teammate positions and the Killer near the hook, helping decide whether you should rescue or keep working.
  • Déjà Vu · Support — Highlights a grouped generator trio so you can leave a finished job for useful repair instead of searching blindly.
  • Premonition · Support — Outside chase, a directional nearby-Killer cue gives an approach decision when no teammate can warn you.

How to Play

Opening

Ask which missing call matters next: hook coverage, the next grouped generator or an approaching Killer. The 10.2 intent system reduces some uncertainty but does not replace these specific reads.

Situation

A teammate is hooked and Kindred shows another survivor nearer the hook or the Killer remaining nearby.

Do this

Let the nearer safe rescuer take the approach while you keep useful repair; move yourself only when the visible team state leaves the rescue uncovered.

Why

The hook aura read prevents duplicated rescue trips, but only while a Survivor remains hooked.

Enabled by

Kindred

Situation

Your generator finishes or you leave a dangerous location and Déjà Vu shows the grouped trio.

Do this

Choose a reachable highlighted generator that advances the objective without dragging the chase into teammates; stop scouting once the destination is clear.

Why

This is a destination cue, not an instruction to ignore immediate hook or Killer pressure.

Enabled by

Déjà Vu

Situation

Outside chase, Premonition triggers while you look toward a nearby Killer.

Do this

Leave the exposed position or pre-position for the approach; do not keep turning for another cue while its cooldown runs.

Why

The brief aura window supplies an early decision, not continuous tracking.

Enabled by

Premonition

Situation

The team already has a safe rescue covered, a work target is known or voice calls answer the same question.

Do this

Return to repair or the urgent rescue instead of detouring for another information read.

Why

The source's main cost is overinvesting in knowledge instead of tempo.

When to stop

  • A cue no longer changes the rescue, repair or safety decision.
  • A coordinated call already supplies the same state and following the aura would abandon a more urgent action.
Perk details

Kindred

Kindred

What it does: While you are hooked, all Survivors see each other's Auras, and while the Killer is within 14/15/16 metres of your Hook, all Survivors see the Killer's Aura. While another Survivor is hooked, you see the Auras of the other Survivors, and while the Killer is within 14/15/16 metres of that hooked Survivor, you see the Killer's Aura.

Activation:

  • Any Survivor is hooked.
  • Ends/deactivates when: The effect ends when no Survivor remains hooked.

Owner: General

Why it is here: Hook events expose teammate positions and the Killer near the hook, helping decide whether you should rescue or keep working.

Déjà Vu

Déjà Vu

What it does: The Auras of the three closest Generators that are grouped together are revealed to you. You repair those Generators 4/5/6% faster.

Activation:

  • Ends/deactivates when: Highlighted Generators update as the closest grouped trio changes.

Owner: General

Why it is here: Highlights a grouped generator trio so you can leave a finished job for useful repair instead of searching blindly.

Premonition

Premonition

What it does: While not being chased by the Killer, whenever you look in the Killer's direction within 32 metres, you are notified and see the Killer's Aura for 3 seconds. Cooldown: 55/50/45 seconds.

Activation:

  • You are not being chased.
  • Look in the Killer's direction while they are within 32 metres.
  • Ends/deactivates when: The Aura reveal ends after 3 seconds.
  • Ends/deactivates when: The perk cannot trigger during its 55/50/45-second cooldown or while you are being chased.

Owner: General

Why it is here: Outside chase, a directional nearby-Killer cue gives an approach decision when no teammate can warn you.

Solo Q and SWF differences

Solo Q

Read the hook and objective state yourself when teammates cannot call it; turn each cue into one decision rather than collecting more auras.

Coordinated SWF

Keep only the information that adds to voice calls; repeated known hook or generator state is usually a poor slot trade.

Weaknesses

  • Multiple information perks can displace direct chase, healing or repair value; voice coordination reduces the shell's distinct advantage.

Common mistakes

  • Both travelling to an obvious hook rescue when the aura already shows coverage.
  • Waiting for a perfect information picture instead of beginning the next useful repair.

Short Description

Replace missing voice information with passive game-state visibility. Explicitly compensates for uncertainty in Solo Q; community builds label this directly.

Overview

Solo-Q Information Shell is a portable package that can attach to several broader builds. Its primary role is Decision support, with secondary emphasis on Rescue, objectives. The canonical reason players run it is Avoid duplicated rescues, find work and read danger.

How It Works

  • Activation: Create or recognize the state in which replace missing voice information with passive game-state visibility. Explicitly compensates for uncertainty in Solo Q; community builds label this directly..
  • Use case: Commit when the likely payoff, avoid duplicated rescues, find work and read danger, is worth the time and slot cost.
  • Payoff: Successful execution changes the match through Teammate/Killer/hook/gen auras.
  • Failure state: The plan fails when its activation is denied, arrives too late, or consumes more team tempo than the resulting benefit returns.

Why It Works

The strategy works by turning Decision support into a favorable exchange of match resources. Its defining mechanisms are Teammate/Killer/hook/gen auras. The payoff the research is trying to capture is Avoid duplicated rescues, find work and read danger. The useful question is not whether the effect looks strong in isolation, but whether the time, positioning, item charges, health states, or perk slots spent to obtain it are repaid by the resulting generator, chase, rescue, or survival tempo.

Winning and Losing States

Winning state: The strategy is generating more team value than it costs in setup, travel, health states, item charges, or perk slots. Its intended payoff is being realized: Avoid duplicated rescues, find work and read danger.

Losing state: The player keeps paying the setup cost after the Killer, map, teammates, or game state have made the activation low-value. That is where specialization turns into opportunity cost.
