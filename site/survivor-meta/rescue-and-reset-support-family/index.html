Hook Rescue / Post-Unhook Reset

Open this approach

Choose when: A hooked teammate needs a reachable unhook and a safe post-unhook reset can preserve their next chase.

Trade-off: The rescuer leaves objectives and can expose both Survivors if the Killer still controls the hook or heal location. Release the reset when it is unsafe.

Dedicated Healer / Triage

Open this approach

Choose when: An injured teammate is reachable and a dedicated healer can restore them without abandoning a critical generator or hook.

Trade-off: Travel and healing take team time; against immediate pressure or anti-heal, hand off or return to the objective instead of chasing every injury.

Protection-Hit / Tank

Open this approach

Choose when: A well-positioned healthy Survivor can take a protection hit that materially keeps a teammate or route alive.

Trade-off: The protector spends a health state and risks a second down. Do not step into a hit when it cannot change the teammate's escape or rescue outcome.

Hook-State Transfer

Open this approach

Choose when: A hooked teammate's hook state is the limiting resource and an eligible Survivor can deliberately transfer one state.

Trade-off: The user inherits hook-state risk and the live perk's injury and Broken cost. Use the child plan only when that exchange is better than an ordinary safe unhook.

Short Description

This page is a navigation and comparison hub for a shared strategic family. This family groups strategies that preserve team hook-state economy and stabilize injured or hooked teammates through rescue, rapid resetting, protection, anti-camp timing, or hook-state redistribution. Its children are too strategically different for one aggregate Power score. The family itself is deliberately unranked because its children use different mechanics, costs, and counterplay.

How the Family Works

The family does not have one gameplay loop. Its children share an objective, but each manipulates different resources and has different activation windows. Use the child comparison below to choose the actual execution plan.

Why the Children Are Not Interchangeable

Different child strategies can target the same high-level outcome while paying for it with different resources. One may depend on an item, another on positioning or communication, and another on a narrow timing window. The correct choice is therefore the child whose activation cost and counterplay fit the team plan, not whichever happens to sit highest in one environment.

Dependencies and Reliability

  • TEAMMATES: The strategy depends materially on teammate state, location, behavior, or willingness to cooperate.
  • POSITIONING: The player must already be in the right place, or move there efficiently, before the value window closes.
  • TIMING: Small timing windows materially separate successful execution from wasted commitment.

Strategic Diagnostics

No unified strategic-diagnostic scores are assigned to this parent family. Its child strategies must be evaluated individually rather than averaged.

Shared Counterplay

The Killer has real counterplay, but shutting the strategy down usually requires deliberate adaptation rather than one automatic answer. The canonical counter note is: support labor can be mistimed or duplicated and healing can be the wrong macro choice.
