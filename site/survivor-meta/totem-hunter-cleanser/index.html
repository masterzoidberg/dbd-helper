Player Guide

Totem Hunter / Cleanser

Find and cleanse a totem when removing a live Hex or converting that cleanse into a useful heal or repair window repays the search time.

Well-supported · Rising

Verdict

Treat cleansing as a response to a visible threat or a planned reward, not a standing detour from repairs.

Solo Q: Follow nearby totem information and abandon a long search when a rescue or useful repair is available.

Coordinated SWF: Tell teammates which Hex or cleanse reward is worth pursuing so several players do not duplicate the same search.

Main weakness: Search and cleanse time can buy nothing against a low-totem match and can consume a totem a teammate wanted to bless.

Solo QD tier
Coordinated SWFD tier

How to Play

Opening

Decide whether the immediate job is removing a dangerous Hex or earning a specific cleanse reward; check what objective or rescue the detour will delay.

A relevant Hex is active and a totem is on a reachable route.

Situation: A relevant Hex is active and a totem is on a reachable route.

Action: Use Small Game's nearby reveal to check that route and cleanse the target if the removal matters now; stop ranging outward when the team needs a rescue or generator completion.

Why: The threat makes the detour valuable, while a broad hunt can surrender more tempo than the Hex costs.

Relevant when: Small Game

Another totem removal is justified after your first cleanse.

Situation: Another totem removal is justified after your first cleanse.

Action: Use Counterforce's farthest-totem reveal as a lead only if a second cleanse is worth its travel; otherwise return to the team objective.

Why: Its speed and information improve a real cleanse chain but cannot make an unnecessary chain valuable.

Relevant when: Counterforce

A safe dull or Hex totem is nearby and a later self-heal or uninterrupted repair period is likely.

Situation: A safe dull or Hex totem is nearby and a later self-heal or uninterrupted repair period is likely.

Action: Cleanse for Inner Strength's locker heal or Overzealous's repair bonus, then take that planned follow-up; skip the totem if the follow-up is unlikely.

Why: The payoff comes after cleansing and still requires a usable locker or repair window.

Relevant when: Inner Strength, Overzealous

No meaningful Hex remains, the team needs an immediate save, or a teammate is using the totem for a Boon.

Situation: No meaningful Hex remains, the team needs an immediate save, or a teammate is using the totem for a Boon.

Action: Leave the totem and resume rescue or repair; coordinate before consuming a planned Boon site.

Why: A finite totem can be worth more as team time or a shared zone than as an isolated cleanse.

When to stop

  • Stop hunting when no Hex or near-term cleanse reward justifies another travel and cleanse cycle.
  • Yield to an urgent rescue or a teammate's planned Boon site rather than finishing a low-value search.
Perk details

Small Game

Small Game

What it does: Within 10/11/12 metres of a Totem, you see its Aura.

Activation:

  • A Totem is within 10/11/12 metres.
  • Ends/deactivates when: The Totem's Aura is no longer revealed after it leaves the tiered range or no longer exists.

Owner: General

Why it is here: Shows nearby totems during an already useful route; use it to shorten a search rather than roam solely for a distant dull totem.

Counterforce

Counterforce

What it does: Your Totem cleansing speed is set to 125%. Each Totem you cleanse adds a stackable 25% cleansing-speed bonus. After each cleanse, the Aura of the Totem farthest from your current location is revealed to you for 10/12/14 seconds.

Activation:

  • Cleansing speed bonus is always active.
  • Cleanse a Totem to gain another stack and reveal the farthest Totem.
  • Ends/deactivates when: Farthest Totem Aura reveal ends after 10/12/14 seconds.

Owner: Jill Valentine

Why it is here: Rewards consecutive cleanses with faster cleansing and a farthest-totem lead; useful when several totems really need removal.

Inner Strength

Inner Strength

What it does: After you cleanse a Totem, Inner Strength activates. While active, hide in a Locker for 10/9/8 seconds to heal yourself by one Health State. It deactivates after healing and cannot be used while you are Broken. Cleansing another Totem can activate it again.

Activation:

  • Cleanse a Totem; blessing a Totem does not activate the perk.
  • While activated, remain hidden inside a Locker for 10/9/8 seconds.
  • You must not be suffering from Broken when attempting to use it.
  • Ends/deactivates when: Deactivates after healing one Health State.

Owner: Nancy Wheeler

Why it is here: Turns a cleanse into a later locker self-heal, provided the user is not Broken and can take a safe locker pause.

Overzealous

Overzealous

What it does: After you cleanse or bless a Totem, Overzealous activates and increases your Generator repair speed by 8/9/10%. The bonus is doubled to 16/18/20% when the activation is from cleansing a Hex Totem. It deactivates after you lose a Health State by any means.

Activation:

  • Cleanse or bless a Totem.
  • Ends/deactivates when: Losing a Health State by any means.

Owner: Haddie Kaur

Why it is here: Converts a cleanse into repair speed until the next lost health state; strongest reason to cleanse when safe repair time follows.

Weaknesses

  • Totems are finite and searching competes with generator, rescue and teammate Boon time.

Common mistakes

  • Leaving a live objective to clear every dull totem without a reward or Hex to answer.
  • Expecting Inner Strength from blessing instead of cleansing, or trying its locker heal while Broken.
  • Keeping a cleanse reward plan after the next health-state loss ends Overzealous.

R05 — Totem Hunter / Cleanser

  • Strategy ID: R05
  • Name: Totem Hunter / Cleanser

  • Trend: RISING
  • Prevalence: Rare–Uncommon

Short Description

Deliberately spend time neutralizing/using Totems. Cleansing/searching, not blessing.

Overview

Totem Hunter / Cleanser is a coherent but situational strategic plan with narrower activation or payoff. Its primary role is Counter-Hex, with secondary emphasis on Healing/objective bonuses. The canonical reason players run it is Answer dangerous Hexes and trigger cleanse rewards.

How It Works

  • Activation: Create or recognize the state in which deliberately spend time neutralizing/using Totems. Cleansing/searching, not blessing.
  • Use case: Commit when the likely payoff, answer dangerous hexes and trigger cleanse rewards, is worth the time and slot cost.
  • Payoff: Successful execution changes the match through Locate and cleanse Totems, convert cleanses into other value.
  • Failure state: The plan fails when its activation is denied, arrives too late, or consumes more team tempo than the resulting benefit returns.

Why It Works

The strategy works by turning Counter-Hex into a favorable exchange of match resources. Its defining mechanisms are Locate and cleanse Totems, convert cleanses into other value. The payoff the research is trying to capture is Answer dangerous Hexes and trigger cleanse rewards. The useful question is not whether the effect looks strong in isolation, but whether the time, positioning, item charges, health states, or perk slots spent to obtain it are repaid by the resulting generator, chase, rescue, or survival tempo.

Winning and Losing States

Winning state: The strategy is generating more team value than it costs in setup, travel, health states, item charges, or perk slots. Its intended payoff is being realized: Answer dangerous Hexes and trigger cleanse rewards.

Losing state: The player keeps paying the setup cost after the Killer, map, teammates, or game state have made the activation low-value. That is where specialization turns into opportunity cost.

Strategic Diagnostics

  • Win Condition Contribution 2/5: Contributes indirectly or only in favorable states.
  • Activation Reliability 3/5: Has workable but inconsistent access.
  • Activation Payoff 2/5: Offers limited value relative to the setup.
  • Denial Risk 2/5: Has relatively little direct counterplay once the conditions are present.
  • Role Commitment 4/5: Often changes the player's role and opportunity costs.
