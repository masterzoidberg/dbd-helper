Player Guide

Hook-Trade / Carry Bodyblock Protector

Use position and, if necessary, one health state to interrupt a hook trade or carrying route only when it can avert a hook; leave when the carry or rescue window closes.

Solo QD tierRecommended
Coordinated SWFB tier

Main weakness: The Killer can drop the carried Survivor, switch targets or take another hook route while the blocker sacrifices health and objective time.

Recommended Build

No required perks

How to Play

Opening

Identify the actual hook or carry path and whether your body can change the outcome before leaving an objective.

Situation

A rescued teammate needs one short opening to leave and you are already positioned to cover that route.

Do this

Occupy the useful path and take a hit only if it lets the teammate escape the immediate recapture. Peel away once their route is open.

Why

Physical presence is the resource; a second down for no route gain loses the trade.

Situation

The Killer carries a teammate toward a known hook and your position can force a meaningful detour.

Do this

Block the relevant path briefly and let the carried teammate exploit the delay; do not chase the Killer across the map for a speculative block.

Why

The payoff is prevented hook tempo, not merely contact with the Killer.

Situation

The Killer changes hooks, drops the carried Survivor or turns onto you.

Do this

Leave the failed line, avoid donating a second down and return to the next rescue or objective assignment.

Why

The recorded counterplay directly removes the value of overcommitted body presence.

When to stop

  • Abort when no reachable route can change the hook or escape result.
  • Exit after a route change or drop rather than following the Killer indefinitely.

Solo Q and SWF differences

Solo Q

Attempt only a clear, immediate save; uncertainty about teammate movement makes extended carry interference especially costly.

Coordinated SWF

Call the intended route and blocker before contact so the carried or rescued teammate can convert the delay and others stay on objectives.

Strengths

  • Good position can exchange one health state for avoided hook tempo.

Weaknesses

  • High positioning and timing cost, especially without communication; the Killer can refuse the expected path.

Common mistakes

  • Arriving after the carry route is already decided.
  • Continuing to bodyblock after the Killer drops the Survivor or switches to the blocker.

Short Description

Interpose between Killer and hook/rescued player. Physical pathing/body presence is the resource.

Overview

Hook-Trade / Carry Bodyblock Protector is a dedicated in-match job that changes how the player allocates time and attention. Its primary role is Rescue denial, with secondary emphasis on Protection hits. The canonical reason players run it is Convert health states into prevented hook tempo.

How It Works

  • Activation: Create or recognize the state in which interpose between Killer and hook/rescued player. Physical pathing/body presence is the resource.
  • Use case: Commit when the likely payoff, convert health states into prevented hook tempo, is worth the time and slot cost.
  • Payoff: Successful execution changes the match through Occupy path, take hits, force awkward carries/trades.
  • Failure state: The plan fails when its activation is denied, arrives too late, or consumes more team tempo than the resulting benefit returns.

Why It Works

The strategy works by turning Rescue denial into a favorable exchange of match resources. Its defining mechanisms are Occupy path, take hits, force awkward carries/trades. The payoff the research is trying to capture is Convert health states into prevented hook tempo. The useful question is not whether the effect looks strong in isolation, but whether the time, positioning, item charges, health states, or perk slots spent to obtain it are repaid by the resulting generator, chase, rescue, or survival tempo.

Winning and Losing States

Winning state: The strategy is generating more team value than it costs in setup, travel, health states, item charges, or perk slots. Its intended payoff is being realized: Convert health states into prevented hook tempo.

Losing state: The player keeps paying the setup cost after the Killer, map, teammates, or game state have made the activation low-value. That is where specialization turns into opportunity cost.
