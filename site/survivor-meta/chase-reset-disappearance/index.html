C08 — Chase Reset / Disappearance

Identity

  • Strategy ID: C08
  • Name: Chase Reset / Disappearance
  • Alternative Names: Lose-the-Killer, vanishing build
  • Structural Classification: META SUBTYPE
  • Current Status: ESTABLISHED

  • Solo Q: B | Power 67 | Ranked | Confidence: MEDIUM
  • Coordinated SWF: B | Power 69 | Ranked | Confidence: MEDIUM
  • Meta Stability: 79 / 100 (STABLE)
  • Trend: STABLE
  • Prevalence: Uncommon

Short Description

Take a hit or break LOS and disappear. Starts with accepted chase, then tries to terminate it abruptly.

Overview

Chase Reset / Disappearance is a specialized branch of a broader meta archetype. Its primary role is Escape chase, with secondary emphasis on Healing/reset. The canonical reason players run it is Turn one LOS break into a full reset.

How It Works

  • Activation: Create or recognize the state in which take a hit or break LOS and disappear. Starts with accepted chase, then tries to terminate it abruptly.
  • Use case: Commit when the likely payoff, turn one los break into a full reset, is worth the time and slot cost.
  • Payoff: Successful execution changes the match through Hide scratch marks/blood/noise after contact.
  • Failure state: The plan fails when its activation is denied, arrives too late, or consumes more team tempo than the resulting benefit returns.

Why It Works

The strategy works by turning Escape chase into a favorable exchange of match resources. Its defining mechanisms are Hide scratch marks/blood/noise after contact. The payoff the research is trying to capture is Turn one LOS break into a full reset. The useful question is not whether the effect looks strong in isolation, but whether the time, positioning, item charges, health states, or perk slots spent to obtain it are repaid by the resulting generator, chase, rescue, or survival tempo.

Winning and Losing States

Winning state: The strategy is generating more team value than it costs in setup, travel, health states, item charges, or perk slots. Its intended payoff is being realized: Turn one LOS break into a full reset.

Losing state: The player keeps paying the setup cost after the Killer, map, teammates, or game state have made the activation low-value. That is where specialization turns into opportunity cost.

Solo Q

The canonical Solo Q evaluation is B / 67 with MEDIUM confidence. That places it as viable and above average in this environment. Power is a comparative research score, not an observed escape percentage.

Coordinated SWF

The canonical Coordinated SWF evaluation is B / 69 with MEDIUM confidence. That places it as viable and above average in this environment. Power is a comparative research score, not an observed escape percentage.

Solo Q vs SWF

SWF Power is 69 versus 67 in Solo Q. Its core payoff is mostly intrinsic to the mechanic, so communication changes execution more than fundamental strength.

Power vs Stability

Current competitive power is moderate to strong. Meta Stability is 79 / 100 (STABLE). Its underlying logic is fairly durable even if particular perks or implementations move. Stability measures structural durability, not confidence and not a forecast of an exact future tier.

Strategic Diagnostics

  • Win Condition Contribution 3/5: Can create meaningful value without carrying the match plan alone.
  • Activation Reliability 4/5: Is available in many ordinary matches.
  • Activation Payoff 3/5: Produces a meaningful but not overwhelming swing.
  • Denial Risk 2/5: Has relatively little direct counterplay once the conditions are present.
  • Role Commitment 3/5: Requires noticeable time or slot commitment.

Perks and Build Structure

Core / Defining Tools

Lucky Break; Iron Will; Quick & Quiet; Lightweight/Dance With Me-type effects

Supporting Tools

Supporting slots should protect the strategy's activation, reduce its opportunity cost, or cover a failure state rather than simply duplicate the same effect.

Flex Options

A flex slot can change without changing strategy identity as long as the core gameplay loop remains intact.

Trap / Outdated Choices

The main trap is overinvestment: adding more same-purpose perks can turn a useful module into dead slots when the Killer or match state refuses the interaction.

Dependencies and Reliability

  • ITEM: An item materially changes access, efficiency, or payoff. Losing or exhausting the item reduces the plan.
  • POSITIONING: The player must already be in the right place, or move there efficiently, before the value window closes.
  • RESOURCE_AVAILABILITY: The plan depends on finite pallets, hooks, charges, chests, totems, or other resources remaining available.

Strengths

  • Turn one LOS break into a full reset
  • The strategic concept has durable underlying logic rather than depending on one fragile interaction.

Weaknesses and Failure Modes

  • Duration/resource limits; tracking powers
  • Item loss or exhausted charges reduce the strategy's ceiling.

Killer Counterplay

Direct denial is relatively limited once the strategy is in position, but the Killer can still reduce value by changing targets, routing, or forcing resource expenditure elsewhere. The canonical counter note is: Duration/resource limits; tracking powers.
