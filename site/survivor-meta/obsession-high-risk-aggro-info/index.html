Player Guide

Obsession / High-Risk Aggro-Info

Accept reciprocal Killer information risk only when your position and team plan can turn the reveal or Obsession link into a useful rotation.

Solo QC tierRecommended
Coordinated SWFB tier

Main weakness: The Killer may exploit your revealed position; becoming the Obsession ends Blood Pact's aura link, while separation ends its post-heal Haste.

Recommended Build

Field Guide recommendation

  • Bound by Obsession · Core — Gives reciprocal Killer aura information when the Killer sees yours; its periodic Obsession reveal deliberately exposes your location, so use it with a safe route.
  • Blood Pact · Support — Offers injury-based partner information and a post-heal movement route while you are not the Obsession; its linked benefits are unavailable when you are the Obsession.

How to Play

Opening

Check who is the Obsession, where the team is working and where you can lead attention without endangering that work.

Situation

You have Bound by Obsession and the Killer can see your aura, including a periodic reveal while you are the Obsession.

Do this

Read the Killer's aura, rotate from an exposed objective toward a defensible route, and tell teammates where the Killer is moving; do not linger beside a teammate who cannot absorb that visit.

Why

The information arrives with a matching location leak that the Killer can act on.

Enabled by

Bound by Obsession

Situation

You are not the Obsession, you or the Obsession is injured, and Blood Pact exposes the partner location.

Do this

Use the injury-based aura link to decide whether a safe partner heal or rotation is practical; after a qualifying heal, stay within Haste range only while the movement benefit serves a real route.

Why

The mutual auras require that you are not the Obsession and either of you is injured; only the post-heal Haste requires the pair to remain within range.

Enabled by

Blood Pact

Situation

You become the Obsession, neither partner remains injured, the pair separates after a qualifying heal, or the Killer refuses your planned chase.

Do this

If Obsession or injury state ends the aura link, stop routing by it; if the pair separates, let the Haste plan go without assuming the aura is gone. Return to repair, rescue or ordinary chase routing when the remaining effect has no useful job.

Why

Obsession and injury state govern the aura link; distance governs the post-heal Haste, and the Killer can still decline the intended chase.

Situation

You are injured or out of safe terrain and a reveal would bring the Killer to the team's active work.

Do this

Leave the work area and take a safer route; if you cannot absorb attention, stop trying to draw it and preserve the team's objective time.

Why

A predictable reveal becomes a liability when no useful chase or information decision follows.

When to stop

  • Stop inviting attention when the exposed route leads through a rescue or generator the team must protect.
  • Stop relying on Blood Pact's auras when you become the Obsession or neither partner is injured; abandon the post-heal Haste route when staying within range costs more than it returns.
Perk details

Bound by Obsession

Bound by Obsession

What it does: Whenever the Killer sees your Aura, you see the Killer's Aura. While the Killer sees your Aura, you Bless and Cleanse Totems, heal, and repair 8/9/10% faster. While you are the Obsession, every 30 seconds the Killer sees your Aura for 4 seconds. At Trial start, you are more likely to become the Obsession.

Activation:

  • The mutual Aura and action-speed effects activate whenever the Killer sees your Aura.
  • If you are the Obsession, the Killer sees your Aura every 30 seconds.
  • Ends/deactivates when: The mutual Aura and action-speed effects end when the Killer stops seeing your Aura.
  • Ends/deactivates when: Each periodic Obsession Aura reveal lasts 4 seconds.

Owner: General

Why it is here: Gives reciprocal Killer aura information when the Killer sees yours; its periodic Obsession reveal deliberately exposes your location, so use it with a safe route.

Blood Pact

Blood Pact

What it does: While you are not the Obsession, if you or the Obsession is injured, you see each other's Auras. When you finish healing the Obsession or they finish healing you, both gain 5/6/7% Haste while within 16 metres of each other; this lasts as long as you remain within that range. While you are the Obsession, if you are hooked, another Survivor becomes the Obsession. At Trial start, you are less likely to become the Obsession.

Activation:

  • You are not the Obsession for the mutual Aura and post-heal Haste effects.
  • You or the Obsession is injured for the Aura link.
  • Complete a qualifying heal while within 16 metres for Haste.
  • If you are the Obsession, being hooked transfers the Obsession to another Survivor.
  • Ends/deactivates when: The Aura link ends when neither you nor the Obsession is injured, or while you are the Obsession.
  • Ends/deactivates when: The post-heal Haste ends when you and the Obsession are no longer within 16 metres of each other; it has no fixed time limit.

Owner: Cheryl Mason

Why it is here: Offers injury-based partner information and a post-heal movement route while you are not the Obsession; its linked benefits are unavailable when you are the Obsession.

Solo Q and SWF differences

Solo Q

Treat the reveal as a cue to relocate or protect your own position; the team may not know why the Killer is arriving.

Coordinated SWF

Agree on a safe attention route and a partner handoff before exposing your location or depending on a linked heal.

Weaknesses

  • Information is reciprocal only in the relevant reveal state; inviting attention can cost a health state or pull the Killer onto teammates.

Killer counterplay

  • The Killer can use the revealed aura to approach the exposed Survivor or decline the intended chase and pressure a weaker teammate.

Common mistakes

  • Taking the periodic reveal near a vulnerable teammate or critical generator without an escape route.
  • Expecting Blood Pact's aura and Haste while you are the Obsession.
  • Continuing to solicit Killer attention after the team has lost the capacity to cover objectives or rescue.

Short Description

Accept information risk to attract or read Killer. Exposure to the Killer is deliberately part of the engine.

Overview

Obsession / High-Risk Aggro-Info is a strategy organized around a specific mechanic or interaction. Its primary role is Killer attention, with secondary emphasis on Aura reading, speed/support. The canonical reason players run it is Turn Obsession state and mutual information into intentional macro.

How It Works

  • Activation: Create or recognize the state in which accept information risk to attract or read Killer. Exposure to the Killer is deliberately part of the engine.
  • Use case: Commit when the likely payoff, turn obsession state and mutual information into intentional macro, is worth the time and slot cost.
  • Payoff: Successful execution changes the match through Reciprocal aura exposure, Obsession swapping/link effects.
  • Failure state: The plan fails when its activation is denied, arrives too late, or consumes more team tempo than the resulting benefit returns.

Why It Works

The strategy works by turning Killer attention into a favorable exchange of match resources. Its defining mechanisms are Reciprocal aura exposure, Obsession swapping/link effects. The payoff the research is trying to capture is Turn Obsession state and mutual information into intentional macro. The useful question is not whether the effect looks strong in isolation, but whether the time, positioning, item charges, health states, or perk slots spent to obtain it are repaid by the resulting generator, chase, rescue, or survival tempo.

Winning and Losing States

Winning state: The strategy is generating more team value than it costs in setup, travel, health states, item charges, or perk slots. Its intended payoff is being realized: Turn Obsession state and mutual information into intentional macro.

Losing state: The player keeps paying the setup cost after the Killer, map, teammates, or game state have made the activation low-value. That is where specialization turns into opportunity cost.
