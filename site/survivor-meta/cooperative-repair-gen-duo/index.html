How to Play

Opening

Before stacking, name the generator that must finish and the job each player will take afterward. If there is no target or split plan, repair separately and preserve map-wide labor.

Situation

Another Survivor is reachable and a selected generator is more valuable finished now than two separate repairs.

Do this

Call the target, confirm the exit and start the cooperative repair only while the team can afford the temporary concentration. Do not stack a safe low-value generator just to activate a perk.

Why

The strategy's payoff is burst on a selected target; the opportunity cost is lost map coverage.

Situation

You and at least one other Survivor are repairing within Prove Thyself's 4-metre range.

Do this

Use the shared 6/8/10% per other Survivor bonus up to 18/24/30% to finish the chosen target, then leave the stack when its completion no longer needs both players.

Why

Prove Thyself has no value on a solo repair and can turn into inefficient stacking when the target is not critical.

Enabled by

Prove Thyself

Situation

A generator is nearly complete and at least one other Survivor is repairing it with Friendly Competition equipped.

Do this

Finish together to activate the live 10% repair bonus for 80/85/90 seconds, then split toward separate useful generators or the next assigned job instead of remaining on the completed machine.

Why

The post-finish bonus repays the stack when it is carried into several next objectives; the point is not to keep everyone together.

Enabled by

Friendly Competition

Situation

A nearby teammate needs healing, an unhook, a gate, a cleanse or another listed non-generator action while Leader is equipped.

Do this

Stay within 10 metres long enough to support that action, then release the support position. Do not keep a teammate near you expecting Leader to speed generator repair.

Why

Leader affects other Survivors and listed actions, but explicitly excludes generator repair and self-benefit.

Enabled by

Leader

Situation

The target is denied, the Killer has forced a chase, the partner leaves or an uncovered generator or rescue is now more valuable.

Do this

Stop the cooperative repair assignment and take the best remaining job. Call the handoff in a coordinated team so the same two players do not keep travelling back to a losing target.

Why

The source counter is reduced map-wide labor; cooperation is only an advantage while the selected payoff remains larger than that cost.

When to stop

  • Abort stacking when a second player is no longer needed to finish the target or when another generator or rescue is more valuable.
  • Leave the target when the Killer can pressure both Survivors before the cooperative payoff is realized; ordinary split repair is the recovery plan.
Perk details

Prove Thyself

Prove Thyself

What it does: While repairing a Generator, increases Repair speed by a stackable 6/8/10% per other Survivor within 4 metres, up to 18/24/30%. Prove Thyself extends its effect to all Survivors within its range. A Survivor can be affected by only one instance of Prove Thyself at a time.

Activation:

  • Repair a Generator with at least one other Survivor within 4 metres for a bonus to apply.
  • Ends/deactivates when: The bonus changes as nearby Survivors enter or leave the 4-metre range and ends when the relevant Generator repair stops.

Owner: Dwight Fairfield

Why it is here: While repairing within 4 metres of other Survivors, share the stackable 6/8/10% per nearby Survivor bonus up to 18/24/30%. Use it for a target worth finishing, not every generator.

Friendly Competition

Friendly Competition

What it does: Whenever you finish repairing a Generator with at least 1 other Survivor, the Survivors who finished repairing it repair 10% faster for 80/85/90 seconds.

Activation:

  • You must complete a Generator while at least one other Survivor is also repairing it.
  • Ends/deactivates when: Each affected Survivor's repair-speed bonus expires after 80/85/90 seconds.

Owner: Thalita Lyra

Why it is here: Finish a generator with at least one other Survivor to give every finisher 10% faster repair for 80/85/90 seconds, then spread the team back across useful objectives.

Leader

Leader

What it does: All other Survivors within 10 m of you gain 20/25/30% action speed for Cleansing, Gate-Opening, Healing, Sabotaging, Unhooking, and Unlocking. The effect lingers for 15 seconds after leaving the radius. A Survivor can be affected by only one instance of Leader at a time; the perk owner does not benefit from their own Leader.

Activation:

  • Another Survivor must be within 10 m of you to receive the action-speed bonus.
  • Ends/deactivates when: For each affected Survivor, the bonus ends 15 seconds after leaving the 10 m radius.

Owner: Dwight Fairfield

Why it is here: Give other Survivors within 10 metres 20/25/30% faster Cleansing, Gate-Opening, Healing, Sabotaging, Unhooking and Unlocking, with a 15-second linger. Leader does not increase generator repair and does not benefit its owner.

Solo Q and SWF differences

Solo Q

Use a visible teammate already on a worthwhile generator as the cooperation cue. If the partner leaves or no longer communicates the target, stop treating the module as a fixed assignment.

Coordinated SWF

Call the target, stack owner and split destinations before committing. Release the stack immediately when the team has extracted the finish bonus or the Killer has made the location inefficient.

Strengths

  • Create a large short objective burst and carry the cooperative finish into a coordinated split.

Weaknesses

  • Stacking reduces map-wide labor and needs another Survivor's presence and behavior.
  • Leader supports nearby listed actions but cannot repair generators faster or benefit its owner.

Killer counterplay

  • The Killer can change targets, pressure the stacked generator or force the partner away so the team pays the cooperation cost without receiving the intended finish.

Common mistakes

  • Putting multiple Survivors on every generator instead of selecting one target worth the temporary labor loss.
  • Staying together after Friendly Competition activates instead of splitting the 10% repair bonus across the next objectives.
  • Expecting Leader to increase generator repair or to speed the perk owner's own actions.

Short Description

Pair on selected generators for tempo/synergy. Requires another repairer as part of the engine.

Overview

Cooperative Repair / Gen Duo is a dedicated in-match job that changes how the player allocates time and attention. Its primary role is Cooperative repair, with secondary emphasis on Team support. The canonical reason players run it is Burst key gens and exploit partner effects.

How It Works

  • Activation: Create or recognize the state in which pair on selected generators for tempo/synergy. Requires another repairer as part of the engine.
  • Use case: Commit when the likely payoff, burst key gens and exploit partner effects, is worth the time and slot cost.
  • Payoff: Successful execution changes the match through Shared repair bonuses triggered by teammates.
  • Failure state: The plan fails when its activation is denied, arrives too late, or consumes more team tempo than the resulting benefit returns.

Why It Works

The strategy works by turning Cooperative repair into a favorable exchange of match resources. Its defining mechanisms are Shared repair bonuses triggered by teammates. The payoff the research is trying to capture is Burst key gens and exploit partner effects. The useful question is not whether the effect looks strong in isolation, but whether the time, positioning, item charges, health states, or perk slots spent to obtain it are repaid by the resulting generator, chase, rescue, or survival tempo.

Winning and Losing States

Winning state: The strategy is generating more team value than it costs in setup, travel, health states, item charges, or perk slots. Its intended payoff is being realized: Burst key gens and exploit partner effects.

Losing state: The player keeps paying the setup cost after the Killer, map, teammates, or game state have made the activation low-value. That is where specialization turns into opportunity cost.
