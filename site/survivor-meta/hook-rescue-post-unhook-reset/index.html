Player Guide

Hook Rescue / Post-Unhook Reset

Own a reachable unhook and the safe reset after it; do not turn the rescue role into automatic hook-side healing.

Well-supported · Rising

Verdict

Take the rescue when your route and timing are sound, then heal only if the Killer has left enough space. Return to an objective when another player already owns the save.

Solo Q: Use hook information to avoid duplicating a teammate's rescue trip, and read the area before starting the post-unhook heal.

Coordinated SWF: Assign one rescuer and a separate objective holder; call whether the saved player can reset safely or should leave the hook area.

Main weakness: Travel, a contested hook, or an unsafe heal can consume more team time than the reset returns.

Solo QA tier
Coordinated SWFA tier

Loadout

Perk guidance

Field Guide recommendation

  • Kindred · Support
  • We'll Make It · Core

Item guidance

Med-Kit · Optional

Optional finite healing resource for a safe reset, not a reason to spend extra time near a contested hook.

How to Play

Opening

Check hook time, Killer position, teammate routes and the remaining objective before leaving your task.

A teammate is hooked and multiple Survivors might leave their tasks.

Situation: A teammate is hooked and multiple Survivors might leave their tasks.

Action: Use the visible hook and teammate positions to decide whether you are the closest safe rescuer. If another player is already committed, keep your objective covered.

Why: Duplicate travel is the recorded failure mode; Kindred supplies hook-time aura information, not a guaranteed safe approach.

Relevant when: Kindred

You can reach the hook without giving the Killer an immediate second down.

Situation: You can reach the hook without giving the Killer an immediate second down.

Action: Unhook, move the saved player away from pressure, and heal them only when the route and cover are safe. Use the post-unhook healing window if available.

Why: We'll Make It activates from your unhook and speeds healing other Survivors; the heal still costs time and position.

Relevant when: We'll Make It

The Killer returns, the saved player is already escaping, or the team needs you elsewhere.

Situation: The Killer returns, the saved player is already escaping, or the team needs you elsewhere.

Action: Separate and hand off the reset; do not chase a hook-side heal just to use a perk timer.

Why: Preserving the rescue matters more than converting every activation into a heal.

When to stop

  • Leave an unsafe hook route to a better-positioned rescuer or a later window.
  • Stop the reset if it exposes both Survivors to immediate pressure.
Perk details

Kindred

Kindred

What it does: While you are hooked, all Survivors see each other's Auras, and while the Killer is within 14/15/16 metres of your Hook, all Survivors see the Killer's Aura. While another Survivor is hooked, you see the Auras of the other Survivors, and while the Killer is within 14/15/16 metres of that hooked Survivor, you see the Killer's Aura.

Activation:

  • Any Survivor is hooked.
  • Ends/deactivates when: The effect ends when no Survivor remains hooked.

Owner: General

Why it is here: Hook auras help identify who can cover the save and whether the Killer remains near the hook; the viewer depends on who is hooked.

We'll Make It

We'll Make It

What it does: Whenever you unhook a Survivor, for 70/80/90 seconds you heal other Survivors 100% faster.

Activation:

  • Unhook another Survivor.
  • Ends/deactivates when: The effect ends after 70/80/90 seconds.

Owner: General

Why it is here: After your unhook, altruistic healing is 100% faster for 70/80/90 seconds; it does not speed your own heal or make an exposed hook safe.

Strengths

  • A well-assigned rescue can preserve hook tempo and create a short, efficient healing window.

Weaknesses

  • Duplicate rescue travel and contested hook healing erase the payoff.

Common mistakes

  • Assuming Kindred makes the hook safe rather than merely showing auras.
  • Treating We'll Make It as a self-heal bonus or a reason to heal under pressure.

A01 — Hook Rescue / Post-Unhook Reset

Identity

  • Strategy ID: A01
  • Name: Hook Rescue / Post-Unhook Reset
  • Alternative Names: Rescuer, hook support
  • Structural Classification: ROLE SPECIALIZATION
  • Current Status: ESTABLISHED
  • Parent Strategy: [A00 — Rescue-and-Reset Support Family]
  • Patch Baseline: 10.2.0
  • Solo Q: A | Power 80 | Ranked | Confidence: MEDIUM
  • Coordinated SWF: A | Power 85 | Ranked | Confidence: MEDIUM
  • Meta Stability: 74 / 100 (STABLE)
  • Trend: RISING
  • Prevalence: Common

Short Description

Own the rescue-and-reset sequence. Unhook plus post-hook stabilization, not generic altruism.

Overview

Hook Rescue / Post-Unhook Reset is a dedicated in-match job that changes how the player allocates time and attention. Its primary role is Unhooking, with secondary emphasis on Healing, info. The canonical reason players run it is Prevent hooks from snowballing.

How It Works

  • Activation: Create or recognize the state in which own the rescue-and-reset sequence. Unhook plus post-hook stabilization, not generic altruism.
  • Use case: Commit when the likely payoff, prevent hooks from snowballing, is worth the time and slot cost.
  • Payoff: Successful execution changes the match through Rescue access plus rapid follow-up heal/protection.
  • Failure state: The plan fails when its activation is denied, arrives too late, or consumes more team tempo than the resulting benefit returns.

Why It Works

The strategy works by turning Unhooking into a favorable exchange of match resources. Its defining mechanisms are Rescue access plus rapid follow-up heal/protection. The payoff the research is trying to capture is Prevent hooks from snowballing. The useful question is not whether the effect looks strong in isolation, but whether the time, positioning, item charges, health states, or perk slots spent to obtain it are repaid by the resulting generator, chase, rescue, or survival tempo.

Winning and Losing States

Winning state: The strategy is generating more team value than it costs in setup, travel, health states, item charges, or perk slots. Its intended payoff is being realized: Prevent hooks from snowballing.

Losing state: The player keeps paying the setup cost after the Killer, map, teammates, or game state have made the activation low-value. That is where specialization turns into opportunity cost.

Solo Q

The canonical Solo Q evaluation is A / 80 with MEDIUM confidence. That places it as strong in this environment; teammate uncertainty reduces some of its ceiling. Power is a comparative research score, not an observed escape percentage.

Coordinated SWF

The canonical Coordinated SWF evaluation is A / 85 with MEDIUM confidence. That places it as strong in this environment; planned teammate behavior reduces wasted commitment. Power is a comparative research score, not an observed escape percentage.

Solo Q vs SWF

SWF Power is 85 versus 80 in Solo Q. Coordination provides a meaningful but not transformative reliability gain.

Power vs Stability

Current competitive power is high. Meta Stability is 74 / 100 (STABLE). Its underlying logic is fairly durable even if particular perks or implementations move. Stability measures structural durability, not confidence and not a forecast of an exact future tier.

Strategic Diagnostics

  • Win Condition Contribution 4/5: Regularly converts good execution into major team tempo.
  • Activation Reliability 4/5: Is available in many ordinary matches.
  • Activation Payoff 4/5: Can create a large tempo or survival swing.
  • Denial Risk 2/5: Has relatively little direct counterplay once the conditions are present.
  • Role Commitment 4/5: Often changes the player's role and opportunity costs.

Perks and Build Structure

Core / Defining Tools

Kindred; We'll Make It; Borrowed Time-type support; Babysitter; Aftercare

Supporting Tools

Supporting slots should protect the strategy's activation, reduce its opportunity cost, or cover a failure state rather than simply duplicate the same effect.

Flex Options

A flex slot can change without changing strategy identity as long as the core gameplay loop remains intact.

Trap / Outdated Choices

The main trap is overinvestment: adding more same-purpose perks can turn a useful module into dead slots when the Killer or match state refuses the interaction.

Representative Builds

The canonical database establishes a strategy framework, not one verified four-perk implementation for this record. Its recorded perk ecosystem is: Kindred; We'll Make It; Borrowed Time-type support; Babysitter; Aftercare. These are descriptive research phrases unless a canonical perk ID appears in a BuildImplementation; they should not be treated as a locked loadout.

Items and Add-ons

Canonical item context: Med-Kit optional. An item is only treated as required when the strategy's dependency data actually says so; otherwise it is an efficiency or access tool rather than the identity of the strategy.

Skill and Execution

Stage 3A does not contain canonical numeric Skill Floor, Skill Ceiling, or Execution Reliability fields, so this page does not invent them. Practically, the strategy asks the player to develop: tight timing windows.

Dependencies and Reliability

  • TEAMMATES: The strategy depends materially on teammate state, location, behavior, or willingness to cooperate.
  • ITEM: An item materially changes access, efficiency, or payoff. Losing or exhausting the item reduces the plan.
  • TIMING: Small timing windows materially separate successful execution from wasted commitment.
  • RESOURCE_AVAILABILITY: The plan depends on finite pallets, hooks, charges, chests, totems, or other resources remaining available.

Strengths

  • Prevent hooks from snowballing
  • The strategic concept has durable underlying logic rather than depending on one fragile interaction.

Weaknesses and Failure Modes

  • Can waste travel/time if teammates duplicate rescue
  • Teammate behavior can create wasted commitment or missed activation windows.
  • Item loss or exhausted charges reduce the strategy's ceiling.

Killer Counterplay

Direct denial is relatively limited once the strategy is in position, but the Killer can still reduce value by changing targets, routing, or forcing resource expenditure elsewhere. The canonical counter note is: Can waste travel/time if teammates duplicate rescue.

Common Mistakes

  • Forcing the strategy after its value window has passed instead of returning to generator or rescue tempo.
  • Spending too much match time proving the build can work rather than asking whether the activation is worth the opportunity cost.
  • Assuming teammates will understand the plan without enough information or coordination.
  • Burning the relevant resource early and then expecting the strategy to function unchanged later.
  • Treating the item as infinite and ignoring charge economy or the risk of losing it.
