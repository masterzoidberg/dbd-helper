Recommended Build

Field Guide recommendation

  • Do No Harm · Core — While healing another Survivor, each of their Hook States adds 30/35/40% heal speed, 3% progress on Great basic healing checks and 5% check occurrence chance.

Item guidance

Med-Kit · Optional

Optional finite healing resource; the hook-state scaling comes from Do No Harm, not the item.

How to Play

Opening

Check the injured player's hook-state danger, travel cost and whether the Killer has left a safe reset window.

Situation

An injured teammate with one or two Hook States is reachable and a heal can protect their next chase.

Do this

Move to a safe reset location and heal that teammate. Use Great basic healing checks when they occur; do not assume a special check earns the same bonus.

Why

Do No Harm scales all three bonuses with the target's Hook States and only applies while healing another Survivor.

Enabled by

Do No Harm

Situation

The injured target has no Hook States, the Killer contests the heal, or an urgent hook or generator needs coverage.

Do this

Hand off the heal or return to the higher-value team job instead of chasing a perk activation.

Why

At zero target Hook States the perk has no bonus, and a denied heal consumes team tempo.

Situation

The heal is complete and the endangered teammate can take the next route.

Do this

Separate into useful assignments; do not keep both players idle looking for another heal.

Why

The benefit is a restored teammate, not a standing team-wide buff.

When to stop

  • Stop if Killer pressure makes the reset likely to cost a down.
  • Release the healer role when the target is no longer reachable or another teammate has the safe heal.
Perk details

Do No Harm

Do No Harm

What it does: While healing another Survivor, for each Hook State they have: you heal 30/35/40% faster, Great basic Skill Checks grant 3% more progress, and the odds of triggering a Skill Check increase by 5%.

Activation:

  • Heal another Survivor; all three bonuses scale with that Survivor's Hook States and do nothing at zero Hook States.
  • Ends/deactivates when: The bonuses apply only while healing another Survivor.

Owner: Orela Rose

Why it is here: While healing another Survivor, each of their Hook States adds 30/35/40% heal speed, 3% progress on Great basic healing checks and 5% check occurrence chance.

Solo Q and SWF differences

Solo Q

Confirm the target is reachable and has a Hook State before leaving an urgent objective or rescue.

Coordinated SWF

Assign one healer to the endangered player and keep the other jobs covered; call when pressure makes the reset unsafe.

Strengths

  • More healing value is concentrated on teammates closest to elimination.

Weaknesses

  • Fresh-patch effectiveness remains provisional; low-hook-state targets and unsafe routes provide little return.

Common mistakes

  • Counting the healer's Hook States instead of the target's.
  • Assuming the Great-check bonus applies to non-basic checks or to self-healing.

Short Description

Overview

Hook-State-Scaled Fast Healing is a strategy organized around a specific mechanic or interaction. Its primary role is Healing others, with secondary emphasis on Skill checks. The canonical reason players run it is Stronger assistance where death risk is greatest.

How It Works

Why It Works

The strategy works by turning Healing others into a favorable exchange of match resources. Its defining mechanisms are Heal speed and skill-check benefits scale with target hook states. The payoff the research is trying to capture is Stronger assistance where death risk is greatest. The useful question is not whether the effect looks strong in isolation, but whether the time, positioning, item charges, health states, or perk slots spent to obtain it are repaid by the resulting generator, chase, rescue, or survival tempo.

Winning and Losing States

Winning state: The strategy is generating more team value than it costs in setup, travel, health states, item charges, or perk slots. Its intended payoff is being realized: Stronger assistance where death risk is greatest.

Losing state: The player keeps paying the setup cost after the Killer, map, teammates, or game state have made the activation low-value. That is where specialization turns into opportunity cost.
