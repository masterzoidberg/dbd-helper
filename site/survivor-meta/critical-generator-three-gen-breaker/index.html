Critical-Generator / Three-Gen Breaker

Repair the generators that shape the next rotation, not merely the nearest ones; preserve enough time and resources to break the dangerous cluster before it becomes the endgame.

Item guidance

Toolbox · Optional

The snapshot calls Toolbox use optional. Use charges only when they materially finish the chosen critical generator; no add-on or fixed item package is established.

How to Play

Opening

At the first rotation, identify the dangerous group of generators and the one target whose completion improves the team's later positions. Record the route and release condition before committing to a long cross-map repair.

Situation

You can choose between several accessible generators and Déjà Vu shows a grouped trio.

Do this

Compare the highlighted trio with hooks, teammate positions and the Killer's pressure. Choose the target that prevents the most dangerous late-game lock, not automatically the closest aura.

Why

Generator selection is the defining mechanic; raw repair speed cannot repair a bad map position.

Enabled by

Déjà Vu

Situation

The selected generator is strategically important and the current route can finish meaningful progress.

Do this

Commit while the target still changes the future rotation, then leave before a forced chase or dangerous approach costs more than completing the target returns. Keep the next safe route visible.

Why

The plan wins by selective commitment, not by defending one generator after its value window has closed.

Situation

You have Fast Track Tokens from an unhook and can reach a Great basic repair Skill Check on the chosen target.

Do this

Spend the held Tokens on the target that still matters, accepting 5% permanent progress per Token. If the cluster has changed or the target is already lost, do not travel only to cash out the burst.

Why

Fast Track converts a prior rescue into permanent target progress, but the value still depends on generator selection.

Enabled by

Fast Track

Situation

A Toolbox can close a meaningful amount of progress on the selected generator and the Killer cannot immediately deny the route.

Do this

Spend charges only on the target whose completion changes the rotation. If the item or target is denied, switch to ordinary repair or the uncovered rescue rather than preserving the old plan.

Why

The item is optional and finite; an item burst on the wrong generator does not solve the cluster.

Enabled by

the Toolbox item

Situation

The Killer controls the route, the target's completion no longer prevents a lock or a teammate needs an urgent rescue.

Do this

Abandon the selected generator and rotate to the next useful objective or rescue. Reassess the cluster after the team state changes instead of paying more time to validate the original call.

Why

The canonical failure is continuing a selective commitment after the payoff no longer repays its positioning cost.

When to stop

  • Abort a cross-map target trip when the route is unsafe, a rescue is uncovered or an accessible generator now changes the rotation more.
  • Release an exhausted target when the Killer has converted it into a losing chase or the team can no longer finish it before the map state moves on.

Solo Q and SWF differences

Solo Q

Use your own cluster and hook information, then choose a target you can actually reach. Do not assume teammates will follow a silent three-generator assignment.

Coordinated SWF

Call the cluster, owner and release cue. Let another player take the next objective when the selected target is denied so the team does not keep paying for a stale macro call.

Strengths

  • Prevent a worse late-game generator position through deliberate target selection.
  • Turn unhooks or optional item charges into progress on the generator that matters most.

Weaknesses

  • The strategy asks for macro knowledge and dangerous positioning rather than only faster repair.
  • A denied route, exhausted item or late target call can make the specialization cost more than broad repair.

Killer counterplay

  • The Killer can pressure the selected cluster, force a long approach or turn the target into a chase before the strategic completion pays.

Common mistakes

  • Repairing the nearest generator without checking whether it leaves the dangerous cluster for last.
  • Crossing the map for an ideal target after its completion no longer changes the future rotation.
  • Saving Fast Track Tokens or Toolbox charges for a target that has already become unsafe or strategically obsolete.
