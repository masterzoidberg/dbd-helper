Overview

Self-Sustain / Self-Heal is a dedicated in-match job that changes how the player allocates time and attention. Its primary role is Healing self, with secondary emphasis on Objective uptime. The canonical reason players run it is Independence and reduced teammate travel.

How It Works

  • Activation: Create or recognize the state in which reset independently when team cannot or should not rotate. Primary beneficiary is the user, unlike healer builds.
  • Use case: Commit when the likely payoff, independence and reduced teammate travel, is worth the time and slot cost.
  • Payoff: Successful execution changes the match through Self-heal access without teammate.
  • Failure state: The plan fails when its activation is denied, arrives too late, or consumes more team tempo than the resulting benefit returns.

Why It Works

The strategy works by turning Healing self into a favorable exchange of match resources. Its defining mechanisms are Self-heal access without teammate. The payoff the research is trying to capture is Independence and reduced teammate travel. The useful question is not whether the effect looks strong in isolation, but whether the time, positioning, item charges, health states, or perk slots spent to obtain it are repaid by the resulting generator, chase, rescue, or survival tempo.

Winning and Losing States

Winning state: The strategy is generating more team value than it costs in setup, travel, health states, item charges, or perk slots. Its intended payoff is being realized: Independence and reduced teammate travel.

Losing state: The player keeps paying the setup cost after the Killer, map, teammates, or game state have made the activation low-value. That is where specialization turns into opportunity cost.

Common Mistakes

  • Forcing the strategy after its value window has passed instead of returning to generator or rescue tempo.
  • Spending too much match time proving the build can work rather than asking whether the activation is worth the opportunity cost.
  • Assuming teammates will understand the plan without enough information or coordination.
  • Reacting only after the key event starts instead of pre-positioning for it.
  • Treating the item as infinite and ignoring charge economy or the risk of losing it.
