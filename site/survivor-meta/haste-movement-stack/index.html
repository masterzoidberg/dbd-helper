Choice: Made for This

What it does: While injured, healing a teammate grants temporary Endurance, and running while Deep Wounded grants tier-scaled Haste.

Why it's here: While Injured, healing another Survivor can grant Endurance for 6/8/10 seconds, and running while suffering from Deep Wound activates its live Haste effect.

Endgame movement layer

Hope

What it does: When the Exit Gates are powered, you move faster until the Trial ends.

Why it's here: Once the Exit Gates are powered, gain 3/4/5% Haste for the remainder of the Trial. Save the movement plan for the endgame state instead of treating Hope as an opening tool.

Local team Haste zone

Boon: Dark Theory

What it does: A Boon Totem grants nearby Survivors 3% Haste that briefly persists after leaving its radius.

Why it's here: Bless a Dull or Hex Totem to create a 24-metre area with 3% Haste and a 2/3/4-second linger after leaving. Reposition when the totem is snuffed or the route leaves the zone.

How to Play

Opening

Before the Trial forces a chase, identify which Haste layer is meant to change your next path and what job the movement protects. If the route has no destination, the speed is only setup cost.

Situation

You are selecting a Haste route before an expected chase, rescue or rotation.

Do this

Check the actual gate: Blood Pact needs the non-Obsession injury and qualifying heal link, Made for This needs its injured or Deep Wound state, Hope needs powered Exit Gates, and Boon: Dark Theory needs an active local totem.

Why

These perks are conditional layers, not one interchangeable movement button.

Enabled by

the Blood Pact or Made for This loadout option, the Hope loadout option, the Boon: Dark Theory loadout option

Situation

Blood Pact is active after the qualifying heal and both Survivors can remain within 16 metres.

Do this

Use the shared Haste to reach the next safe position or rescue route, then separate when staying together no longer helps. Do not chase a partner across the map to preserve the effect.

Why

Blood Pact's Haste lasts while the pair remains within range; proximity is both its payoff and its cost.

Enabled by

Blood Pact

Situation

You are Injured and Made for This can supply its current running or post-heal condition.

Do this

Heal another Survivor only when the Endurance window protects a real route, and use the Deep Wound running Haste to create distance rather than looping in place. Stop relying on it when the state ends or a Conspicuous Action removes Endurance.

Why

Made for This has separate Injured, healing and Deep Wound gates; being equipped does not make its movement effect unconditional.

Enabled by

Made for This

Situation

The Exit Gates are powered and Hope is active.

Do this

Use the persistent 3/4/5% Haste to choose a gate, rescue or escape line with a clear destination. Do not spend the early Trial planning around a bonus that does not exist yet.

Why

Hope is a one-time endgame activation for the remainder of the Trial.

Enabled by

Hope

Situation

Boon: Dark Theory is active and the next movement route passes through its 24-metre area.

Do this

Use the 3% Haste to cross the selected route, then account for the 2/3/4-second linger after leaving. If the Killer snuffs the totem or the job moves outside the zone, leave the old plan.

Why

The Boon is local and can be denied; defending an irrelevant totem is not movement value.

Enabled by

Boon: Dark Theory

Situation

A condition is missing, the route has no destination, the Killer has anti-Haste pressure or an urgent generator or rescue is uncovered.

Do this

Stop protecting the movement setup and take the best ordinary route or team job. Do not keep a partner nearby, a Boon active or an injured state solely to preserve a low-value speed plan.

Why

The canonical weakness is setup and denial; the strategy loses when the conditions consume more tempo than the altered path returns.

When to stop

  • Abort a Haste detour when its trigger is unavailable, the next route cannot use the speed or a generator or rescue needs immediate coverage.
  • Release the stack when the Killer has forced a different path or the team is paying more positioning and communication cost than the movement changes.
Perk details

Blood Pact

Blood Pact

What it does: While you are not the Obsession, if you or the Obsession is injured, you see each other's Auras. When you finish healing the Obsession or they finish healing you, both gain 5/6/7% Haste while within 16 metres of each other; this lasts as long as you remain within that range. While you are the Obsession, if you are hooked, another Survivor becomes the Obsession. At Trial start, you are less likely to become the Obsession.

Activation:

  • You are not the Obsession for the mutual Aura and post-heal Haste effects.
  • You or the Obsession is injured for the Aura link.
  • Complete a qualifying heal while within 16 metres for Haste.
  • If you are the Obsession, being hooked transfers the Obsession to another Survivor.
  • Ends/deactivates when: The Aura link ends when neither you nor the Obsession is injured, or while you are the Obsession.
  • Ends/deactivates when: The post-heal Haste ends when you and the Obsession are no longer within 16 metres of each other; it has no fixed time limit.

Owner: Cheryl Mason

Why it is here: When you are not the Obsession and either of you is injured, finish healing the Obsession—or let the Obsession finish healing you—within 16 metres to give both Survivors 5/6/7% Haste while you remain within range.

Made for This

Made for This

What it does: Activates while you are in the Injured State. While active, completing a Healing action on another Survivor grants Endurance for the tier duration; that Endurance ends early if you perform any Conspicuous Action. While active and suffering from Deep Wound, running grants the tier-specific Haste bonus.

Activation:

  • Be in the Injured State.
  • Complete a Healing action on another Survivor to trigger Endurance.
  • Be suffering from Deep Wound and running to receive Haste.
  • Ends/deactivates when: The Endurance effect ends after its tier duration or early when you perform a Conspicuous Action.
  • Ends/deactivates when: The Haste effect applies only while running and suffering from Deep Wound; the perk otherwise remains available while injured.

Owner: Gabriel Soma

Why it is here: While Injured, healing another Survivor can grant Endurance for 6/8/10 seconds, and running while suffering from Deep Wound activates its live Haste effect.

Hope

Hope

What it does: Once the Exit Gates are powered, Hope activates and grants you 3/4/5% Haste for the remainder of the Trial.

Activation:

  • The Exit Gates must be powered.
  • Ends/deactivates when: The effect ends when the Trial ends.

Owner: General

Why it is here: Once the Exit Gates are powered, gain 3/4/5% Haste for the remainder of the Trial. Save the movement plan for the endgame state instead of treating Hope as an opening tool.

Boon: Dark Theory

Boon: Dark Theory

What it does: Bless a Dull or Hex Totem. Survivors within 24 metres gain 3% Haste; the effect lingers for 2/3/4 seconds after leaving its range.

Activation:

  • Bless a Dull or Hex Totem.
  • Be within 24 metres for the main Haste effect.
  • Ends/deactivates when: Effect ends after leaving range and the tiered linger time, or when the totem is snuffed.

Owner: Yoichi Asakawa

Why it is here: Bless a Dull or Hex Totem to create a 24-metre area with 3% Haste and a 2/3/4-second linger after leaving. Reposition when the totem is snuffed or the route leaves the zone.

Solo Q and SWF differences

Solo Q

Use only the Haste conditions you can verify yourself. An uninjured or unavailable partner, an unlit endgame state or a snuffed Boon cannot be assumed by the rest of the team.

Coordinated SWF

Call the partner, Boon route and release cue before moving so the team does not hold an obsolete formation. Coordination improves the provisional engine but does not remove its state gates.

Strengths

  • Change chase spacing, rescue approach or endgame routing when the right Haste condition is active.

Weaknesses

  • The plan is rare and exposed to setup, state gates, proximity, Haste reduction or caps and anti-Haste Killer tools.
  • Multiple movement slots can be inactive at once while still displacing objective or rescue tools.

Killer counterplay

  • The Killer can force a different route, deny the qualifying state, contest the Boon area or use anti-Haste tools so the planned movement exchange no longer pays.

Common mistakes

  • Treating Blood Pact, Made for This, Hope and Boon: Dark Theory as unconditional speed sources.
  • Following a partner or defending a Boon after the route has moved elsewhere.
  • Using movement to prolong a losing chase instead of returning to the generator, rescue or endgame job the speed was meant to protect.

Short Description

Chain or overlap movement buffs to alter pathing. Built around the Haste mechanic rather than one Exhaustion burst.

Overview

Haste / Movement Stack is a strategy organized around a specific mechanic or interaction. Its primary role is Mobility, with secondary emphasis on Rescue, endgame. The canonical reason players run it is Unusual rotations and chase spacing.

How It Works

  • Activation: Create or recognize the state in which chain or overlap movement buffs to alter pathing. Built around the Haste mechanic rather than one Exhaustion burst.
  • Use case: Commit when the likely payoff, unusual rotations and chase spacing, is worth the time and slot cost.
  • Payoff: Successful execution changes the match through Multiple conditional Haste sources.
  • Failure state: The plan fails when its activation is denied, arrives too late, or consumes more team tempo than the resulting benefit returns.

Why It Works

The strategy works by turning Mobility into a favorable exchange of match resources. Its defining mechanisms are Multiple conditional Haste sources. The payoff the research is trying to capture is Unusual rotations and chase spacing. The useful question is not whether the effect looks strong in isolation, but whether the time, positioning, item charges, health states, or perk slots spent to obtain it are repaid by the resulting generator, chase, rescue, or survival tempo.

Winning and Losing States

Winning state: The strategy is generating more team value than it costs in setup, travel, health states, item charges, or perk slots. Its intended payoff is being realized: Unusual rotations and chase spacing.

Losing state: The player keeps paying the setup cost after the Killer, map, teammates, or game state have made the activation low-value. That is where specialization turns into opportunity cost.
