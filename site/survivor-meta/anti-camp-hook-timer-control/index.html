Player Guide

Anti-Camp / Hook-Timer Control

Buy a short hook-timer pause when immediate rescue is unsafe, then use that time to arrange a real save.

Well-supported · Stable

Verdict

Use Reassurance only when a hooked teammate is under sustained pressure and the pause creates an actionable rescue window; it is not a substitute for unhooking.

Solo Q: Check whether another rescuer can use the extra time before spending your own travel on a six-metre approach.

Coordinated SWF: Call the pause and designate the next rescuer so the team does not wait through the timer without a plan.

Main weakness: A six-metre activation route can be dangerous, and a pause is wasted if the Killer leaves or no teammate converts it into a rescue.

Solo QC tier
Coordinated SWFB tier

How to Play

Opening

Read hook stage, Killer pressure, your approach route and who will perform the eventual save.

The Killer controls the hook, immediate rescue risks a down, and you can safely reach six metres.

Situation: The Killer controls the hook, immediate rescue risks a down, and you can safely reach six metres.

Action: Activate Reassurance and tell the next rescuer the pause is active. Use the extra 20/25/30 seconds to reposition or continue useful work, then take a real rescue window.

Why: The perk pauses the Sacrifice Process and, in Struggle Phase, Struggle Skill Checks; it does not free the hooked Survivor.

Relevant when: Reassurance

The Killer has left or a safe unhook is already available.

Situation: The Killer has left or a safe unhook is already available.

Action: Take or hand off the rescue now instead of detouring into Reassurance range.

Why: The module changes timing only when hook pressure makes immediate rescue worse.

The pause is running but the planned rescuer cannot reach or the Killer changes position.

Situation: The pause is running but the planned rescuer cannot reach or the Killer changes position.

Action: Reassign the save immediately; if no safe conversion exists, return to an objective and reassess before the hook stage advances.

Why: One use per Survivor per Hook Instance means the same user cannot simply repeat the pause on that hook.

Relevant when: Reassurance

When to stop

  • Do not force a six-metre approach that exposes you to a down.
  • Stop waiting once the Killer leaves and a safe unhook is available.
Perk details

Reassurance

Reassurance

What it does: While within 6 metres of a hooked Survivor, press Active Ability to pause that Survivor's Sacrifice Process for 20/25/30 seconds. This also pauses Struggle Skill Checks if that Survivor is in the Struggle Phase, and highlights the hooked Survivor's outline in white to you. Reassurance can be used only once per Survivor per Hook Instance.

Activation:

  • Be within 6 metres of a hooked Survivor and press Active Ability.
  • Ends/deactivates when: The pause and outline effects end after 20/25/30 seconds.

Owner: Rebecca Chambers

Why it is here: Within six metres of a hooked Survivor, Active Ability pauses their Sacrifice Process for 20/25/30 seconds, once per Survivor per Hook Instance.

Strengths

  • Can preserve a hook stage long enough to coordinate a safer rescue.

Weaknesses

  • Range, one-use-per-hook-instance limit and dependence on a subsequent rescue restrict value.

Common mistakes

  • Treating the timer pause as a rescue or as repeatable on the same Hook Instance.
  • Approaching a hook after the Killer has already left just to trigger the perk.

Short Description

Buy time instead of forcing bad immediate rescue. Manipulates rescue timing rather than rescue speed.

Overview

Anti-Camp / Hook-Timer Control is a portable package that can attach to several broader builds. Its primary role is Hook-state preservation, with secondary emphasis on Rescue coordination. The canonical reason players run it is Counter proxy/camp pressure without suicidal trade.

How It Works

  • Activation: Create or recognize the state in which buy time instead of forcing bad immediate rescue. Manipulates rescue timing rather than rescue speed.
  • Use case: Commit when the likely payoff, counter proxy/camp pressure without suicidal trade, is worth the time and slot cost.
  • Payoff: Successful execution changes the match through Pause/extend hook-state pressure or coordinate delayed rescue.
  • Failure state: The plan fails when its activation is denied, arrives too late, or consumes more team tempo than the resulting benefit returns.

Why It Works

The strategy works by turning Hook-state preservation into a favorable exchange of match resources. Its defining mechanisms are Pause/extend hook-state pressure or coordinate delayed rescue. The payoff the research is trying to capture is Counter proxy/camp pressure without suicidal trade. The useful question is not whether the effect looks strong in isolation, but whether the time, positioning, item charges, health states, or perk slots spent to obtain it are repaid by the resulting generator, chase, rescue, or survival tempo.

Losing state: The player keeps paying the setup cost after the Killer, map, teammates, or game state have made the activation low-value. That is where specialization turns into opportunity cost.

Skill and Execution

Stage 3A does not contain canonical numeric Skill Floor, Skill Ceiling, or Execution Reliability fields, so this page does not invent them. Practically, the strategy asks the player to develop: hook-state awareness, clear callouts and role coordination, tight timing windows.

Dependencies and Reliability

  • TEAMMATES: The strategy depends materially on teammate state, location, behavior, or willingness to cooperate.
  • COMMUNICATION: Voice or structured communication materially improves timing, assignment, or information sharing.
  • OPPONENT_BEHAVIOR: The Killer can change the strategy's value by refusing the interaction, changing targets, or altering timing.
  • TIMING: Small timing windows materially separate successful execution from wasted commitment.
  • RESOURCE_AVAILABILITY: The plan depends on finite pallets, hooks, charges, chests, totems, or other resources remaining available.

Strengths

  • Counter proxy/camp pressure without suicidal trade
  • The strategic concept has durable underlying logic rather than depending on one fragile interaction.

Weaknesses and Failure Modes

  • Range/timing restrictions; no value if Killer leaves hook
  • Teammate behavior can create wasted commitment or missed activation windows.
  • Without coordination, assignment and timing become less reliable.

Killer Counterplay

Direct denial is relatively limited once the strategy is in position, but the Killer can still reduce value by changing targets, routing, or forcing resource expenditure elsewhere. The canonical counter note is: Range/timing restrictions; no value if Killer leaves hook.

Common Mistakes

  • Forcing the strategy after its value window has passed instead of returning to generator or rescue tempo.
  • Assuming teammates will understand the plan without enough information or coordination.
  • Burning the relevant resource early and then expecting the strategy to function unchanged later.
